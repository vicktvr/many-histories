Reading the size

A sense of prominence

Height follows the dates. Width adds an approximate editorial view of a history’s reach, influence and lasting impact. A long, influential empire therefore occupies more space.

Selected empires also widen and narrow across their expansion and decline. These are broad historical phases, not annual measurements.

How widths are calculated

Every timeline history has a reviewed display tier. Those weights share space among contemporary histories. Regional lanes expand and contract in the left-to-right order shown above the chart. Thin blocks retain a minimum width; overlap and broad context frames receive separate treatment.

Duration contributes through height only, so it is not counted twice. Changing neighbours also change a block’s width: the outline is not a direct graph of territory or power.

Scores and phase multipliers are editorial choices, not a scholarly consensus, territorial measurements, population estimates or a ranking of peoples. Early, regional and living cultures can have major importance that a political comparison cannot capture. Equal widths removes this weighting.
